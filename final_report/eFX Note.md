*RELEVANCE TO eFX* 

Although the experiment uses a stylized Treasury bargaining game, its structure is analogous to electronic FX execution. The dealer’s private cost represents a dealer’s reservation price; the client’s limit represents its willingness to pay; and the client’s counteroffer represents a bid back to the dealer. The disclosed band is a controlled proxy for post-trade data, such as recent fill levels across liquidity providers. Capture is a price-improvement proxy, while rejected counteroffers are analogous to missed fills. With the band, a rational client keeps 11% more of the surplus; the LLM client gained nothing reliable, and a quarter of its bids sat on the lowest disclosed price, where most were rejected. The experiment therefore studies a protocol risk relevant to eFX: giving an automated execution agent more market information does not guarantee better execution if the agent anchors on displayed prices instead of updating its estimate of execution risk. This is a synthetic eFX analogue, not evidence about live-market performance.
